15 Best Luxury HolidayDestinations for 2027

By Solace Society Travel Design· November 2026· 12 min read

Fifteen places our designers return to, not because they rank, but because they reward. With best seasons, signature stays, and the one experience in each that justifies the journey.

Every year, the travel industry publishes lists. This is not that kind of list. The fifteen destinations below are the places Solace Society's travel designers find themselves composing most often for 2027, chosen not for their Instagram arithmetic but for something harder to measure: how a place makes a person feel after the luggage is unpacked.

Some are famous and still worth it. Some are quiet and worth more. All of them, composed properly, with the right season, the right room and the right door opened, will rearrange something in you. That is the entire point of the exercise.

§How these fifteen were chosen

Our designers evaluate destinations on four axes, which also happen to be the four pillars of the Society: travel (does the journey itself reward the hours), wellness (does the place restore rather than deplete), culture (is there depth beneath the surface), and access (can doors be opened that ordinarily stay shut). A destination that scores strongly on two or more earns its place.

We also weigh 2027 specifically: new properties opening, capacity changes, and the slow shift of discerning travellers toward longer, slower, single-country journeys rather than grand tours of highlights.

01

Kyoto, Japan

The quiet capital of slow travel

If Tokyo is Japan's answer to the future, Kyoto is its reply to the present: a city that rewards those who slow down enough to notice. The temples are famous; what is less discussed is the texture between them: tea houses behind unmarked doors, gardens composed for a single bench, artisans who have spent sixty years learning to do one thing perfectly.

For 2027, Kyoto rewards the traveller who stays five nights, not two. The first days are for the landmarks; the rest are for the city that exists once the guidebooks close. A private tea ceremony with a master who accepts no walk-ins, a dawn walk through Fushimi Inari before the gates have company, dinner at a counter with seven seats and no menu.

Best months
March–April (blossom) · November (maples)
Ideal length
5–7 nights, unhurried
Signature stay
A riverside ryokan with private onsen
  • Best for: culture travellers, honeymoon second chapters, anyone fatigued by fast travel.
  • The one experience: a private dawn visit to a Zen garden, arranged before public hours.
02

The Maldives

Still water, and nothing that needs doing

The Maldives has been called the world's most reliable luxury destination, and the description undersells it. Reliability is not the point; stillness is. No other destination on this list so completely removes the question of what to do next. The answer is always: nothing, beautifully.

What separates a composed Maldives journey from a booked one is the atoll. North Malé is convenient; Baa and Dhaalu are worlds. Private seaplane transfers, a sandbank dinner for two, a house reef that begins at your steps. For 2027, the newer southern atolls offer the rarest currency in the Maldives: true privacy at sane prices.

Best months
December–April (dry, settled)
Ideal length
4–7 nights
Signature stay
An overwater villa with house reef and butler
  • Best for: honeymoons, recovery travel, anyone who needs the world to stop for a week.
  • The one experience: dinner on a private sandbank as bioluminescence gathers in the shallows.
03

Santorini, Greece

The caldera hour, earned

Santorini's problem is its fame; its secret is that fame can be outrun. The cruise crowds occupy the caldera between eleven and four. Everything before and after belongs to those who stay. And staying well in Santorini means a suite carved into the cliff with a plunge pool facing the volcano, not a room facing the car park.

A composed Santorini includes the island's second act: the black-sand beaches of the east, the Bronze Age city of Akrotiri, Assyrtiko wine grown in baskets on volcanic soil, and a catamaran evening that leaves the harbour precisely as the crowds arrive back at theirs.

Best months
May–June · September–October
Ideal length
4–6 nights, paired with one more island
Signature stay
A cave suite with private plunge pool in Imerovigli
  • Best for: celebrations, first-time Greece, couples who want beauty without effort.
  • The one experience: a private catamaran dinner sail as the caldera turns gold.
04

Bora Bora, French Polynesia

The overwater dream, in the flesh

Every overwater bungalow ever photographed descends from this one lagoon. Bora Bora remains the archetype: Mount Otemanu rising like a set piece, water in shades no monitor renders, and a hospitality culture, the Polynesian concept of haere mai (you are welcome), that no hotel school teaches.

The journey is long, and that is part of the alchemy: no one arrives at Bora Bora casually. Compose it with Tahiti at either end, add a helicopter circuit of the atoll, and allow a full day to do nothing on a motu the resort will prepare and then leave entirely to you.

Best months
May–October (dry season)
Ideal length
5–7 nights, with Tahiti
Signature stay
An overwater bungalow suite facing Otemanu
  • Best for: honeymoons that must be the best, bucket-list travellers, romantics.
  • The one experience: a private motu picnic: the island, a table, and no one else.
05

The Swiss Alps

Wellness at altitude

The Alps are where luxury travel goes to recover its health, literally. The sanatorium tradition of the nineteenth century survives in a string of resorts that treat wellness as medicine rather than marketing: Zermatt, Gstaad, Arosa, Davos. Fresh air at altitude, glacier light, thermal water, and a national obsession with punctuality that makes the whole apparatus feel effortless.

In winter, the composition is skiing and fireside; in summer, hiking between alpine lakes and dining on terraces reached only on foot. Either way, the chalet is the point: a private one, with a chef who knows your preferences by the second evening.

Best months
December–March (snow) · June–September (summer)
Ideal length
5–8 nights
Signature stay
A staffed private chalet above Zermatt
  • Best for: wellness travellers, families, winter romantics.
  • The one experience: sunrise on the Gornergrat railway, before the first train of tourists.
06

The Amalfi Coast, Italy

La dolce vita, properly aged

The Amalfi Coast has been famous for seventy years and fashionable for all of them, yet its pleasures are stubbornly local: lemons the size of fists, cliff-path walks between villages, swims from rocks rather than beaches, and long lunches that end at the hour other countries call dinnertime.

Compose it from the sea: a private gozzo with a captain who knows which cove is empty on which afternoon, and stay above the fray in a suite at one of the old-guard hotels where the staff remember returning guests across decades. Ravello for the gardens, Positano for the theatre, and one night at a vineyard on the slopes of Vesuvius to finish.

Best months
May–June · September
Ideal length
5–7 nights
Signature stay
A clifftop suite in Ravello or Positano
  • Best for: food and wine travellers, anniversaries, artists at heart.
  • The one experience: lunch at a family trattoria reachable only by boat.
07

Marrakech, Morocco

A thousand years behind one door

Marrakech operates on the principle of the threshold. Outside: the Medina, a millennium of commerce in sensory overload. Inside: a riad of tile, plaster, water and shade so complete that the city becomes a rumour. The luxury of Marrakech is architectural, and the traveller who understands this is rewarded beyond measure.

Beyond the city, the Atlas Mountains and the Agafay stone desert compose into three movements: souks and cuisine in the Medina, a night in a luxury camp under the Atlas, and recovery in a private garden villa. The contrast is the point: this is a destination about borders, and crossing them well.

Best months
October–April
Ideal length
5–7 nights with the Atlas or desert
Signature stay
A private historic riad with courtyard pool
  • Best for: culture travellers, design lovers, winter sun.
  • The one experience: dinner in a palmeraie garden reached by lantern-lit horse carriage.
08

Bali, Indonesia

The island that invented retreat

Bali has hosted the world's wellness pilgrimages for decades, and the genuine article is still there beneath the industry: an island where ritual is daily life, where the gorge at sunrise is a temple, and where a week of structured rest can undo a year of unstructured everything.

The 2027 composition pairs two Balis: Ubud's jungle retreats, with serious programmes, private villas and rice-terrace walks, and the southern coast's clifftop calm at Uluwatu. Between them, the island's private estate villas, where a staff of ten serves two guests and the infinity pool ends at the jungle's edge.

Best months
April–October (dry season)
Ideal length
7–10 nights
Signature stay
A private estate villa in Ubud or Uluwatu
  • Best for: wellness travellers, long-stay travellers, yoga sceptics (convertible).
  • The one experience: a private water purification at a temple outside the tourist circuit.
09

Iceland

The planet, showing off

Iceland is the only destination on this list where the landscape itself is the luxury. Waterfalls that rearrange your sense of scale, black beaches, glaciers you can walk inside, and a capital that punches far above its weight in restaurants. The accommodation, glass lodges and geothermal retreats, exists to keep you warm while the planet performs.

Compose it privately: a super-jeep and a guide who reads weather the way sommeliers read wine, the Retreat at the Blue Lagoon after the day-trippers leave, and the northern lights as a possibility rather than a promise, which is, of course, what makes them unforgettable when they arrive.

Best months
September–March (lights) · June–August (midnight sun)
Ideal length
5–7 nights
Signature stay
A glass lodge or the Retreat at Blue Lagoon
  • Best for: expedition-minded couples, photographers, winter romantics.
  • The one experience: a private glacier cave visit, far from the group circuits.
10

Patagonia, Chile

The edge of the world, in comfort

There are grander mountains on the map, but no landscape on Earth concentrates drama like Patagonia: granite towers rising sheer from golden steppe, turquoise lakes fed by glaciers, wind with a personality. It is the ultimate answer to the question "where can we feel genuinely far away", and in 2027 its lodges answer the follow-up: "without sleeping badly."

The composition centres on Torres del Paine, explored on horseback with gauchos, by private boat on glacial lakes, or on foot with a guide who times the trails around the crowds. Evenings return to lodges where the food, wine and fires are considerably better than the wilderness requires, which is precisely the luxury.

Best months
November–March (austral summer)
Ideal length
7–9 nights
Signature stay
An all-inclusive expedition lodge in Torres del Paine
  • Best for: hikers and non-hikers travelling together, adventure honeymooners.
  • The one experience: sunrise at the base of the towers, reached before the park wakes.
11

Cape Town, South Africa

A city with a mountain for a heart

Cape Town is the most naturally gifted city on this list: Table Mountain rising directly from the sea, two oceans, wine country thirty minutes inland, and a food scene that has quietly become one of the world's most interesting. It is also the world's best-combined destination: safari, winelands and city in a single, logical journey.

The city composition: a villa above Clifton's four beaches, a private cable car ascent at sunset, a day in the Cape Winelands with a sommelier who grew up on these farms, and dinner where the chef decides. Then north, to the private reserves of the Kruger, where the Big Five complete the picture.

Best months
November–March (austral summer)
Ideal length
5–7 nights, plus 3–4 on safari
Signature stay
A staffed villa above Clifton beaches
  • Best for: food and wine travellers, city-plus-nature itineraries.
  • The one experience: a private winelands day ending dinner at a working wine farm.
12

Bhutan

The last Himalayan kingdom

Bhutan does not compete for tourists; it simply sets terms: a daily composition that includes guiding, transport and accommodation. The result is the least crowded profound destination on Earth. Monasteries clinging to cliffs, valleys where Gross National Happiness is policy, and a pace of life that recalibrates your own within days.

The journey is a sequence of lodges across valleys: Paro, Thimphu, Punakha and Gangtey, each with private guides, hot-stone baths, and walks through villages that receive few enough visitors to still be curious about you. The climb to Tiger's Nest, done at dawn before the day-trippers, is the single most rewarding morning in Asian travel.

Best months
March–May · October–November
Ideal length
7–10 nights
Signature stay
A circuit of valley lodges with private guide
  • Best for: spiritual travellers, hikers, second-time Asia visitors.
  • The one experience: dawn at Tiger's Nest with the monastery to yourself.
13

Tuscany, Italy

The villa life, done properly

Tuscany's idea of luxury is older than the word: a stone villa among cypresses, a long table under a pergola, a kitchen garden, and a cook who has been making the same perfect ragù for thirty years. It is the destination for travellers who find hotels glamorous but homes restorative.

The composed Tuscany is a week, not a weekend. A private villa in the Val d'Orcia with staff, a driver for Siena and Florence timed around the crowds, a day with a truffle hunter and his dog, and one evening at a vineyard where the family has farmed the same rows since the Renaissance. Children are welcome; time slows accordingly.

Best months
May–June · September–October
Ideal length
7 nights minimum
Signature stay
A staffed historic villa in the Val d'Orcia
  • Best for: families, groups of friends, food travellers, long stays.
  • The one experience: a private dinner in a villa garden, cooked from its own garden.
14

Kenya: The Maasai Mara

The greatest show on Earth, privately

A safari is the last form of travel where nature holds the script and humans merely attend. And the Maasai Mara is its premiere theatre. The Great Migration crosses these plains; lions learn the vehicles; and from a private conservancy camp, the show runs at hours the public parks cannot reach: night drives, bush breakfasts, walks with Maasai guides.

Composition is everything in safari. The right conservancy, the right month, a private vehicle with a guide who has studied these animals for decades: the difference between watching wildlife and knowing it. End with three nights on the Kenyan coast or a Lamu island dhow to wash the dust off in style.

Best months
July–October (Migration) · January–March (calmer, greener)
Ideal length
4–6 nights safari, plus coast
Signature stay
A private conservancy tented suite
  • Best for: wildlife obsessives, photographers, multigenerational families.
  • The one experience: a dawn balloon safari ending in a champagne bush breakfast.
15

Dubai, UAE

The future, with room service

Dubai divides opinion precisely because it refuses to be modest. And that, composed correctly, is its virtue. Nowhere else can you breakfast above the clouds, lunch on a desert reserve with oryx, and dine underwater by evening. It is the world's most efficient spectacle, and the luxury traveller's art is knowing which spectacles deserve the hours.

The 2027 composition pairs the vertical city with its older soul: a suite above Downtown, a private desert safari ending in a Bedouin-style camp that is considerably more comfortable than the original, a morning in the Al Fahidi historic district, and one deliberately excessive dinner. Dubai rewards travellers who treat it as theatre rather than destination.

Best months
November–March
Ideal length
3–5 nights, ideally combined
Signature stay
A high-floor suite facing the Burj Khalifa
  • Best for: short-haul luxury, city lovers, families, stopover mastery.
  • The one experience: a private desert dinner under stars, an hour from the skyline.

§A note on how to choose

The destinations above reward opposite virtues. Kyoto and Bhutan reward patience; Dubai and Santorini reward curation. The Maldives rewards the art of doing nothing; Patagonia punishes it. If a choice must be made, our designers default to one rule: choose the place whose ordinary days you would enjoy, not just its extraordinary one. The photograph lasts a minute. The week lasts a lifetime of recall.

And if the answer is more than one: a safari that ends on an island, Kyoto joined to the Maldives. That is not indecision. It is a journey, and it is precisely what private travel design exists to compose.

§Questions, answered

There is no single best destination, only the best match. For overwater seclusion the Maldives and Bora Bora lead; for culture and cuisine, Kyoto and the Amalfi Coast; for wellness, Bali and the Swiss Alps; for raw wilderness, Patagonia and Kenya. The right choice depends on the season, the occasion and the traveller.

For peak-season windows: December to April in the Maldives, June to September in the Alps, safari high season in Kenya, composition should begin 6 to 9 months ahead. Shoulder seasons often deliver better value and quieter access with 3 to 4 months of lead time.

As a working range, bespoke luxury journeys typically begin around USD 800–1,200 per person per night for five-star composition, rising with private guides, charter flights and rare-access experiences. A travel designer can calibrate precisely once the brief is understood.

The destinations our designers compose most for honeymoons are the Maldives, Bora Bora, Santorini, Kyoto and Bali, combining privacy, ceremony-worthy settings and unhurried pacing. Mauritius and the Amalfi Coast suit couples who want culture alongside the sea.

Bali's jungle retreats, the Swiss Alps' sanatorium tradition, Maldives island stillness and Iceland's geothermal rituals are the four corners of wellness composition in 2027, from structured programmes to pure pause.

Yes. Multi-country composition: safari in Kenya followed by an island pause, or Kyoto joined to the Maldives. That is where private travel design earns its keep. Private charters and carefully sequenced routing keep the journey effortless.
